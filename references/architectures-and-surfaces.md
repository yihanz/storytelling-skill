# Architectures and surfaces

Use this reference to select and adapt meaning architecture. Treat every pattern as a starting hypothesis. The source, audience, and required outcome decide.

## 1. Architecture families

### Narrative

Use when change through time, agency, intention, pressure, choice, or consequence is the material.

Possible movement:

```text
grounded situation -> goal or expectation -> real pressure or gap -> choices and consequences -> current meaning or next possibility
```

The actor may be a person, team, customer, product, organization, system, or idea. Do not make the author the hero by default. Conflict can be internal, structural, environmental, or absent. Ongoing stories may end in a live question rather than resolution.

### Causal explanation

Use when the audience needs a manipulable model of why or how something happens.

Possible movement:

```text
orienting frame -> relevant parts -> relations -> mechanism -> boundary conditions -> consequences or use
```

Show multiple causes, feedback loops, moderators, and uncertainty when present. A diagram often carries the system better than a character arc.

### Argument or decision

Use when the audience must judge a conclusion, recommendation, policy, investment, or tradeoff.

Possible movement:

```text
decision and stakes -> governing claim -> evidence and warrant -> strongest countercase -> comparison -> conclusion and implication
```

Make the evidence layer independently inspectable. Do not use narrative transport to conceal weak support or make one case stand in for the distribution.

### Demonstration

Use when observable performance is more persuasive than description.

Possible movement:

```text
relevant prior state -> intervention or product in use -> observable change -> proof and boundary -> next action
```

Keep the product, behavior, or mechanism visible. Do not make the testimonial or cinematic treatment the proof.

### Learning progression

Use when the audience needs a new mental model and the ability to transfer it.

Possible movement:

```text
useful frame or question -> model -> worked example -> contrast or failure case -> application -> retrieval or transfer check
```

Calibrate scaffolding to prior knowledge. A story may supply context or a memorable case, but it must not displace the concept.

### Instruction or operating flow

Use when correct action matters more than immersion.

Possible movement:

```text
goal and prerequisites -> state or decision check -> action -> verification -> exception or recovery
```

Sequence only when sequence is real. Use a decision tree, checklist, or schema when branching matters.

### Orientation, map, or reference

Use when the artifact must support non-linear navigation, repeated use, or later retrieval.

Possible movement:

```text
entry point -> map of the system -> owners and relationships -> current state -> routes by user need
```

Use narrative sparingly on an orientation page. Put mutable facts in their live owners. Do not make a maintained workspace depend on remembering a chronological story.

### Manifesto

Use when the artifact must make a worldview, identity, or direction coherent and felt.

Possible movement:

```text
observed reality -> conviction -> principles -> consequences -> invitation or commitment
```

Name a real antagonist only when one exists. A tension, old assumption, or structural condition often serves better than an invented enemy.

## 2. Surface adaptations

### Live presentation

Design an experience over time. Give each slide or visual beat a clear audience job, while the speaker carries precision, connective logic, and human presence. Keep slides legible at a glance. Do not turn them into a teleprompter or omit evidence the audience must judge.

### Read-alone pitch deck

Make the logic self-contained. A common business sequence is problem, consequence, insight, solution, proof, model, risk, and ask, but reorder it when the decision requires another path. Include quantities, assumptions, risks, and the exact ask. Do not leave theatrical gaps that only a presenter can fill.

### Elevator pitch

Create recognition quickly: the audience's problem or desire, the distinct outcome or mechanism, a proof signal when available, and the next step. Use an origin story only when it is the differentiator. Prefer a clear sentence to a compressed slogan.

### Manifesto

Build intellectual and emotional coherence, not a sales funnel. Preserve voice, plurality, consequences, and honest tension. Let principles change how the reader sees or acts. Avoid founder mythology, generic enemies, and repeated declarations of importance.

### Partner or employee onboarding

Build both belonging and a shared operating model: purpose, current reality, how the system works, where the person fits, decision rights, examples of judgment, first actions, and reliable reference paths. Stories can transmit tacit knowledge and culture. They do not replace policies, ownership, exceptions, or canon.

### Complex project or corpus briefing

Orient with the current map, then use causal or chronological history only where it explains the present. Preserve decisions, conflicts, version boundaries, unresolved questions, and what matters next. A newcomer should leave with both the big picture and a route back to evidence.

### Business plan

Make the operating thesis judgeable: need, solution, market, model, execution, evidence, economics, risk, dependencies, and milestones. Human stakes can establish relevance, but they cannot substitute for validation or distributional evidence.

### Landing page

Design scan order as a series of small state changes: recognition, distinct value, specificity, proof, fit, friction removal, and action. Give each section a clear job. Keep the copy native to the buyer and brand. Do not force a long chronological story onto a surface built for scanning.

### Commercial script

Within the real time limit, establish a relevant tension or desire, show the product or idea doing meaningful work, deliver the supported payoff, and make the action clear. Allocate image, sound, dialogue, supers, and product demonstration deliberately. Emotion must not hide what is being claimed.

### Storyboard

Specify how perception changes frame by frame. For each frame, identify the audience job, visible action, continuity, sound or words, evidence source, duration when material, and transition. Remove decorative shots that survive only because they look good.

### Notion or another durable workspace

Use story to orient only where it improves entry and shared understanding. Let information architecture own the working surface: canonical homes, hierarchy, relationships, status, ownership, retrieval, and next-use fields. Keep mutable state in live records. Never turn every database description or section into explanatory doctrine.

### Technical or scientific explanation

Prefer a system model, mechanism, comparison, or worked example. Preserve exact terms, quantities, causal limits, uncertainty, and failure modes. Use an analogy only when its structural mapping is accurate, and name where it breaks.

## 3. Hybrid design

Many strong artifacts combine architectures because the audience has several jobs.

Examples:

- A pitch can open with one grounded customer situation, shift to population evidence, explain the mechanism, then present the decision and ask.
- Onboarding can use a short origin story for meaning, a system map for orientation, worked cases for judgment, and a reference layer for execution.
- A complex briefing can lead with current state, use a selective chronology to explain it, then switch to a decision architecture.
- A product page can use scan-first argument, a demonstration, and a short customer narrative without becoming a chronological essay.

Make each mode change legible. Do not blend anecdote, evidence, and inference until their roles become indistinguishable.

## 4. Moves and their conditions

Contrast, curiosity, concretization, figure, and emotion are the moves most often reached for by habit. Each does real work through a specific mechanism, and each has a characteristic way of failing. Use one because its mechanism is the one the audience needs, and stop when the material stops supplying it.

### Contrast

**Mechanism.** Perception is comparative. A difference becomes legible only against something it differs from, and a quantity, a state, or a change that is presented alone gives the audience nothing to measure it by.

**Use it** where a real difference is load-bearing: before and after, current and proposed, this population and that one, what was expected and what occurred, what the system does and what it does not. Contrast also lives between things on the same side, which is often where the more informative difference sits — two options that share a goal and diverge on method usually teach more than a hero and a villain.

**It fails** when the opposition is manufactured, when the two sides are not actually comparable, or when a real difference of degree is presented as a difference of kind. Sharpening a contrast past what the evidence supports is a factual defect, not a stylistic one.

### Curiosity gap

**Mechanism.** Interest can be produced by making the audience aware of a gap in what they know. Surprise opens attention; an unanswered question sustains it, because an open question is uncomfortable in a way a mere fact is not.

**Use it** when a real gap exists and the artifact genuinely closes it: pose the question the material actually answers, let the audience feel it, then answer it. The move earns its place by improving the eventual understanding, not by delaying it.

**It fails** as withholding. If the gap is manufactured, if the answer is trivial once revealed, or if the delay serves the artifact's drama rather than the audience's comprehension, the audience pays attention twice and is repaid once. In reference and operating surfaces the move is nearly always wrong: someone who needs an answer now should not have to earn it.

### Concretization

**Mechanism.** Concrete things are easier to hold, picture, and recall than abstractions, and an abstraction the audience cannot instantiate is one they cannot check.

**Use it** to give an abstract claim at least one instance the audience can inspect, and to give every material quantity a reference frame they already possess — a rate, a duration, a comparison, a familiar equivalent.

**It fails** in two directions. Concretizing away real abstraction destroys meaning: when the general form *is* the idea, an instance is not a substitute for it. And a reference frame is itself a claim — the wrong comparator misleads more efficiently than the raw number would, because it feels like help.

### Figure: metaphor and analogy

**Mechanism.** A figure transfers structure from something the audience knows to something they do not, which is why a good one teaches quickly.

**Use it** when the structural mapping is genuinely accurate, and name the point where it breaks. A figure that is not load-bearing is decoration wearing the costume of explanation.

**It fails** as cliché. The first figure that comes to mind is usually one the audience has seen enough times to stop seeing — the handshake, the mountain, the journey, the iceberg, the lightbulb. A stock figure signals that the thinking stopped at the first available option, and audiences read that signal accurately.

### Emotion

**Mechanism.** Emotional material is attended to and recalled more readily than neutral material. That is exactly why it is dangerous: salience and importance are different properties, and the move raises only one of them.

**Use it** where the material genuinely carries human stakes and where feeling those stakes is part of understanding the situation correctly.

**It fails** when emotional weight and evidentiary weight come apart. A vivid minor case will outcompete the central finding in memory, and the audience will leave with a distorted model built from true parts. Whenever emotional intensity exceeds a unit's actual importance, the fix is to reduce the intensity or raise the support, not to accept the imbalance because it is engaging.

## 5. No-story branch

Prefer a direct or non-narrative structure when the primary job is:

- exact execution or safety;
- audit, provenance, or readback;
- comparison across repeated fields;
- lookup and repeated retrieval;
- a current status or decision record;
- formal technical, legal, or data representation;
- a simple answer with no meaningful progression.

The absence of a story is a successful storytelling decision when it produces the strongest audience outcome.
