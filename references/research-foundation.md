# Research foundation

Use this reference to inspect the evidence behind the Skill, resolve a contested principle, or revise the Skill. It is not a checklist to repeat in normal use.

The ledger below is the Skill's empirical base. Each entry states what the study found, what the Skill does with it, and links the source.

It is not the whole of the Skill. Much of the craft guidance elsewhere — when to reach for a move, how to run an audit, how to sequence a working process — is practitioner heuristic, accountable to use rather than to experiment. That material is not represented here and should not be treated as though it were. Where a heuristic and a finding conflict, the finding governs and the heuristic is the thing that changes.

## Research ledger

### Orientation and prior knowledge

- Relevant context given before an ambiguous passage improved comprehension and recall much more than the same context supplied afterward. Use the minimum frame that lets the audience interpret what follows. Do not turn this into a mandatory executive summary. [Bransford and Johnson, 1972](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(72)80006-9)
- Experts underestimated novice completion times and resisted debiasing. Model audience knowledge explicitly. Do not infer it from demographics. [Hinds, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.5.2.205)
- A meta-analysis of 60 experimental studies found that useful instructional assistance varied with prior knowledge and domain. Adapt scaffolding at runtime. [Tetzlaff et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102142)

### Mental models, causality, and sequence

- Readers represented events across time, space, protagonist, causality, and intentionality. Use only the dimensions the material needs. [Zwaan, Langston, and Graesser, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x)
- Membership in a causal chain and the number of causal connections predicted recall, summary inclusion, and judged importance. Find the real causal or dependency spine, but never invent one. [Trabasso and van den Broek, 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(85)90049-X)
- Story schemata and hierarchical relations influenced recall and reconstruction. Familiar progression can help, but the evidence does not establish one universal plot. [Mandler and Johnson, 1977](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(77)90006-8)
- People can feel that they understand a mechanism more deeply than they do. Test whether the artifact exposes the mechanism and boundaries needed for correct use, not merely whether it feels coherent. [Rozenblit and Keil, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2605_1)

### Signaling, visuals, and cognitive load

- Previews, headings, and causal pointer words improved transfer in printed, spoken, and animated science lessons. Use cues to reveal real structure. [Mautone and Mayer, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.2.377)
- A meta-analysis found a small-to-medium benefit for signals connecting text and pictures, especially for lower-prior-knowledge learners. Do not signal everything. [Richter, Scheiter, and Eitel, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2015.12.003)
- Pictures helped when they expressed the useful model rather than merely mirrored the text. Give visuals an information job. [Glenberg and Langston, 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-596X(92)90008-L)
- Sequential visual comprehension depends on learned conventions. Check whether the audience can decode the visual grammar. [Cohn, 2020](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-019-01670-1)
- Interesting but irrelevant details reduced main-idea recall and transfer across four experiments. Remove story elements that compete with the communication job. Relevant richness is not clutter. [Harp and Mayer, 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.90.3.414)

### Concreteness and language

- Concrete texts were generally more comprehensible, interesting, and memorable across several genres, with effects varying by genre. Prefer concrete substance when it carries meaning. [Sadoski, Goetz, and Rodriguez, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.92.1.85)
- Needlessly complex wording reduced perceived intelligence through lower processing fluency. Preserve real technical complexity; remove lexical performance. [Oppenheimer, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1178)

### Persuasion and its risks

- Absorption in a story increased story-consistent beliefs and reduced detection of false notes. Use immersion intentionally, and keep evidence inspectable when judgment matters. [Green and Brock, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701)
- A meta-analysis of 65 experimental pairs found no significant overall advantage for narrative over statistical evidence. Effects depended on context. [Xu, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/10410236.2022.2137750)
- An earlier meta-analysis found statistical evidence stronger for beliefs and attitudes, while narrative evidence was stronger for intentions. Choose the form from the intended outcome. [Zebregs et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1080/10410236.2013.842528)
- Across three experiments, embedding facts in stories could improve or weaken persuasion, and immersion sometimes reduced differentiation between strong and weak arguments. Narrative must not launder evidence quality. [Krause and Rucker, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167219853845)
- Narrative science communication can improve engagement while implying inevitability and normative judgments that have not been argued. Preserve causal and ethical scrutiny. [Dahlstrom, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1320645111)
- Narrative visualization uses selection, encoding, annotation, and sequence rhetorically. Audit what the view foregrounds, suppresses, or implies. [Hullman and Diakopoulos, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2011.255)

## Unsupported universal rules

Nothing in the corpus above establishes any of these as a universal requirement: hooks, protagonists, conflict, climax, three acts, a hero's journey, surprise, anecdotes, emotion, visuals, one idea per slide, fixed length, readability levels, or "show, do not tell." Several were never tested by it at all, so treat their absence here as unexamined rather than refuted. What the corpus does contradict is treating memorability, engagement, persuasion, comprehension, truth, and decision quality as interchangeable.

The durable principle is narrower and stronger: construct the most faithful audience-usable model, then choose the architecture and channels that improve the required audience transition without weakening truth.
